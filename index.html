ORBIT LAB

Drag anywhere on the sky to launch a body: where you press is where it starts, and the direction and length of the drag is its velocity. Scroll to zoom, right-drag to pan. A body that leaves the view is still out there — Fit View brings everything back on screen.

Orbital elements

Measured from each body's position and velocity relative to the heaviest one, which is the two-body approximation every orbit here is judged against. a is the semi-major axis, e the eccentricity — 0 is a circle, below 1 an ellipse, 1 or more an escape. The predicted period is Kepler's third law, T = 2π√(a³/μ); where a third body matters, it will not be what you observe, and that discrepancy is the three-body problem in one column.

Gravity is softened — the force uses √(r² + ε²) with a small ε, shown in the readout above — so a dead-centre collision produces a fast slingshot instead of a division by zero and an infinite velocity. Energy and momentum are computed with the same softened potential, which is why the readout below stays honest even during a close pass.
